## Recommended post

AI became powerful enough to suggest that software engineers might disappear.

The first demonstrations made that conclusion look reasonable. AI could analyze requirements, write code, create tests, investigate failures, and produce documentation. A complete application could emerge from one conversation.

Long-running projects reveal a different reality.

AI works inside finite context. Sessions lose continuity. Plausible answers can still be wrong. Several agents increase output without creating shared ownership, independent review, or accountability.

The human role does not return to typing every line of code. It moves toward product judgment, project continuity, architecture, quality assurance, operations, and responsibility.

This article explains why capable human and AI collaboration can carry projects that once required a larger team, what practices make that collaboration dependable, and how an early project template is trying to turn those lessons into a reusable starting point.

## Short version

AI can write much of the software. It still cannot hold the whole growing project, verify every plausible answer, or own the consequences.

The programmer's future may be less about producing code and more about combining product, project, architecture, QA, and operational judgment with AI execution.

This article explains the limits, the opportunity, and one early attempt to make the collaboration repeatable.
